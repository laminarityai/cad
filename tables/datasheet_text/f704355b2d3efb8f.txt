CORROSION RESISTANCE
T ypes 316 and 316L exhibit improve chloride corrosion 
resistance when compared to T ype 304 due to the 
molybdenum addition to the steel. This allows for 
acceptable corrosion protection to marine atmospheres 
when pitting corrosion is concerned. They also provide 
good chemical resistance to most agents used in the 
paper, petroleum, food and dairy industries. They are 
useful in sulfuric acid environments up to 150 °F (60 °C) 
when concentrations are below 5%. They show excellent 
corrosion resistance to acetic, formic, phosphoric,and 
tartaric acids, as well as some concentrations and 
temperatures of bromide and iodide solutions. When 
welding operations are used, the lower carbon T ype 316L 
allows for reduced risk of intergranular corrosion caused 
by chromium carbide precipitation.  
HEAT TREATMENT
Annealing: Heat to 1900 – 2100 °F (1038 – 1149 °C), then 
rapidly quench. 
COLD WORKING
Due to higher nickel content, these grades work harden at 
a lower rate than T ype 304. In the annealed condition, they 
exhibit excellent ductility and may be readily roll formed, 
deep drawn, and bent. Annealing is essential to restore 
ductility and to lower hardness for subsequent forming 
operations. Severely formed parts should be annealed to 
remove stresses.
