## Moisture conditioning and nylon fits
<!-- keys: moisture absorption, humidity, dry as molded, conditioned, swelling, nylon -->
Moisture exposure can change polymer dimensions and mechanical behavior; the response depends on the material and exposure history. Keep dry and conditioned data distinct. For a fitted part, identify the humidity or immersion conditions during both measurement and use.

Source: Mitsubishi Chemical's dimensional-stability guidance, Reduced moisture absorption. This does not prescribe one conditioning procedure for all nylon grades.

## Thermal expansion and clearance
<!-- keys: thermal expansion, hot fit, cold fit, dimensional change -->
Heating and cooling can change polymer dimensions enough to affect mating clearances. Consider both components and their temperature histories when explaining a fit. A nominal CAD clearance describes the modeled geometry, not its service-temperature behavior.

Source: Mitsubishi Chemical's dimensional-stability guidance, Minimal thermal expansion.

## Creep stress relaxation and service temperature
<!-- keys: sustained load, creep, stress relaxation, HDT, heat deflection temperature -->
Under sustained stress, deformation can increase with time; under constrained deformation, stress can relax. Short-duration strength data do not establish long-term retention of shape or clamping force. Heat-deflection temperature is a test result, not a complete continuous-service limit or a modulus at the operating temperature.

Supplemental source: [Mitsubishi Chemical Engineering Design Guide](https://www.mcam.com/mam/44687/MCG-ENGG-Design-Guide-LIT-EN-230530.pdf), Designing with Plastics. Obtain data appropriate to the grade, load, duration and temperature before drawing a long-term conclusion.

## Machining stress and evidence needed
<!-- keys: residual stress, warpage, machining heat, close tolerance -->
Stock processing and machining influence internal stress and dimensional stability. Tight tolerances may require an appropriate stock condition and machining procedure, not simply a smaller drawing tolerance.

Source: Mitsubishi Chemical's dimensional-stability guidance, Low internal stress.
