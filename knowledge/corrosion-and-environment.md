## Exposure and corrosion table limits
<!-- keys: concentration, impurities, chemical exposure, corrosion rates -->
Outokumpu's supplemental [corrosion tables](https://www.outokumpu.com/en/products/corrosion-tables) describe results for clean, annealed materials with normal microstructure. Concentration, temperature and impurities affect applicability; laboratory results do not directly predict every service environment.

Catalog application: record the actual medium, concentration, temperature, exposure duration and material condition. A favorable entry for a pure solution does not approve an unspecified mixture.

## Chloride pitting and crevice corrosion
<!-- keys: salt water, chlorides, pitting, crevices, passive film -->
Outokumpu explains that stainless protection depends on a passive surface layer. Local breakdown can produce pitting or crevice attack, particularly in chloride environments. Resistance to uniform corrosion does not establish resistance to localized attack.

Catalog application: include trapped-liquid locations and joint geometry when requesting evidence. Compare the exact grades in [304](cliffs-304.md), [316](cliffs-316.md) and [303](stainless-303.md); a stainless label alone does not establish suitability.

## Galvanic contact between dissimilar metals
<!-- keys: galvanic corrosion, electrolyte, dissimilar metal joint -->
Outokumpu describes galvanic attack when electrically connected dissimilar materials share an electrolyte. The less noble material can corrode faster than when uncoupled. Passive stainless can therefore aggravate corrosion of an attached less noble metal.

Catalog application: identify both contacting materials, electrical continuity and wet exposure. Request joint-specific evidence rather than evaluating each material independently. See [fastened joints](fastened-joints-and-inserts.md).

## Polymer chemical resistance and stress cracking
<!-- keys: chemical compatibility, environmental stress cracking, polymer exposure -->
Ensinger's supplemental [chemical resistance guidance](https://www.ensingerplastics.com/en-us/plastic-material-selection/chemical-resistant) identifies temperature, concentration and exposure time as important variables. Amorphous plastics can be vulnerable to environmental stress cracking.

Catalog application: qualify the selected grade under representative load and exposure; a generic chemical-resistance label is insufficient. See [polymer service conditions](polymer-service-conditions.md) and [elastomer selection](elastomer-selection.md).

## Evidence needed before accepting service suitability
<!-- keys: surface condition, corrosion test, compatibility evidence -->
Outokumpu's corrosion mechanisms and the supplemental tables address different questions. Catalog policy: preserve source conditions and identify which failure mechanism the evidence covers. Record surface condition, fabrication history and exposure with the selected material.

This catalog supplies research context, not a corrosion-life prediction or environmental qualification. Unresolved compatibility remains unknown; use [material identity](material-identity.md) to keep the evaluated condition explicit.
