## Identity and stock condition
<!-- page: 10 -->
<!-- keys: low carbon steel, cold finished bar, cold drawn, stock form -->
Alro describes 1018 as a general-purpose low-carbon steel. Its cold-finished-bar section distinguishes processing such as drawing, turning and grinding, which affects surface, dimensional accuracy and properties. Keep the actual supplied condition with the grade.

Source location: PDF page 10, printed section page 1-2, Cold Finished Carbon Bars.

## Forming and case hardening
<!-- page: 10 -->
<!-- keys: bending, cold forming, crimping, swaging, case hardening -->
Alro identifies cold forming and bending as uses for 1018 and notes its case-hardening suitability. Severe bends can require stress relief to avoid cracking. Select the forming and treatment route for the part rather than assuming an untreated stock property also describes the finished condition.

See [CNC guidance](cnc-design-guidance.md) for machining considerations.

## Distinguish 1018 from generic mild steel
<!-- page: 23 -->
<!-- keys: A36, mild steel, commercial quality, hot rolled -->
Alro lists 1018, A36 and commercial-quality hot-rolled stock separately. An informal description such as mild steel does not establish which specification was supplied. Do not treat those designations as aliases for one material.

Source location: PDF page 23, printed section page 1-15. The existing [commercial steel sheet entry](cliffs-hot-rolled-cs-type-b.md) also has its own product identity.

## Property evidence and unknown inputs

Catalog policy: require the material specification, stock dimensions and condition when selecting numerical evidence. A surface-hardening result is not an automatic bulk yield strength.
