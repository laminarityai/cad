## Standards edition and legacy table identity
<!-- keys: ISO 2768:2026, ISO 2768-1:1989, withdrawn, replacement, standard edition -->
The existing `ISO 2768-f`, `ISO 2768-m` and `ISO 2768-c` table keys retain their ISO 2768-1:1989 basis. Their short names do not mean the latest edition. This guide does not relabel or replace those numerical rows.

Status checked 2026-11-12: [ISO's publication record](https://www.iso.org/standard/85741.html) lists ISO 2768:2026 as published on 2026-11-09 and ISO 2768-1:1989 as withdrawn. Its public scope covers linear and angular sizes and excludes other dimensional specifications such as distances. The public description does not establish equivalence with the legacy numerical tables.

[ISO 22081:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/72514.html) separately replaced ISO 2768-2:1989. These are distinct publication histories. Use the edition specified by the drawing; determining a migration requires the applicable documents. This summary uses public metadata and scope, without reproducing standard text or tables.

## General versus individually specified tolerances
<!-- keys: drawing, general tolerance, individual tolerance, manufacturing process -->
The cited AmesWeb reference describes legacy ISO 2768-1 general linear tolerances for dimensions without individual tolerance indications, on parts produced by material removal or sheet-metal forming. Their use is stated on the drawing. An explicitly specified dimensional tolerance is a separate instruction.

The product exposes selected linear classes for plain-hole requirements. This limited use is not certification that an entire part or drawing conforms to the standard, and does not establish a printing process capability.

## ISO 2768-f fine
<!-- keys: linear fine, symmetric tolerance, plus minus, ISO 2768-f -->
Exact tolerance-table key: `ISO 2768-f`, the fine linear class from the legacy ISO 2768-1:1989 table. Read the permissible deviation from the row containing the nominal size. The deviation is in millimetres and applies symmetrically above and below nominal.

Unlike an H hole class, this class is not a zone starting at nominal. Do not confuse a general-tolerance class with the close, medium or coarse clearance-hole series.

## Range boundaries and limits of coverage
<!-- keys: size range, boundary, units, chamfer, broken edge, angular, GD&T -->
Product-table snapshot: these classes cover nominal sizes from 0.5 mm through 400 mm. The first interval includes its lower endpoint; later intervals exclude the lower endpoint and include the upper endpoint. The source says "Values are in millimetres."

The source also describes broken-edge and chamfer tolerances. Those are separate tables and are not the linear values used for the product plain-hole requirement. Very coarse, angular, geometric and inch-fit tolerances are not added by this catalog entry.
